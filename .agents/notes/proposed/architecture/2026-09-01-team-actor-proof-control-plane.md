# Agent Note: Team actor proof control plane

Status: proposed

English | [中文](2026-09-01-team-actor-proof-control-plane.zh.md)

## Problem

Team commands currently accept raw participant identifiers, activation records, or system names. Those values identify a durable subject but do not prove that the current caller holds that subject's authority. Host and SDK management routes can therefore derive a human id from Team state or omit an actor entirely, while the Hub falls back to the Team root grant. Session headers have the same problem: they route a command to a Team but are not an authenticated human principal.

## Proposal

The shipped product-authenticated human consumer is recorded in [Authenticated product-principal Team control](../../implemented/architecture/2026-09-04-authenticated-product-principal-team-control.md). This proposal retains the broader activation and system-proof decision scope.

Define a non-serializable `TeamActorProof` issued only by a real authority source and resolved by the Hub before policy or durable acceptance. A proof is opaque runtime state, never a Team journal, checkpoint, Session event, WebSocket frame, RPC parameter, or model input. The Hub projects a resolved durable attribution into closure and audit facts only after it verifies the proof's Team scope, issuer lease, and current human or activation relationship.

Existing authority sources mint the first proofs: an activation-bound local or WebSocket Link mints an activation proof after it verifies the exact durable binding; TeamRun, scheduler, activation controller, recovery, and retention mint narrowly scoped system proofs for their owned operations. A future Host authentication provider mints human proofs only after mapping an authenticated product principal to one exact active human Participant. No route derives a proof from a Session header, participant id, or request body.

Until a route has such a source, it fails closed. `/goal` remains status-only, and Host/SDK generic management writes become unavailable rather than treating a selected human or a fabricated system name as authority. Read, list, watch, and audit calls remain available.

## Current product-run boundary

The temporary product boundary does not revoke a process's own TeamRun operations. Host keeps `team.create`, `team.start`, and `team.postInput`/`team.waitFinal`/`team.cancel` for a TeamRun the local process still owns; the SDK keeps `team/create` and `team/wait-final`/`team/cancel` for its tracked runtime-owned run. `team-run` retains the source-scoped proof for final receipt only while that run remains current, so a detached request for either wait or cancellation is a generic management write and rejects. Host `session.cancel` for a Team-provenanced coordinator also rejects rather than turning its Session header or roster entry into authority. Terminal archive is the narrow exception: only a TeamRun that itself completed or cancelled a Team retains an in-memory terminal owner, and Host or the same SDK server may route that owner through `archiveTerminal()`; an SDK removes its own terminal record after success, while a Host owner permits an idempotent retry until TeamRun disposal. Resume, member, channel, and task writes remain unavailable; reads, lists, watches, audits, quiescence, and metrics retain their existing scope.

Root Team creation now follows the same process-owned boundary. `team-run` retains one exact `TeamSystemRootCreationProof` only while the Hub creates its root, and the Hub binds that proof to the complete root payload before policy and again after opening the new stream. The first Team record, projection, and checkpoint retain only derived `{ kind: 'system', name: 'team-run' }` provenance as `createdBy`; a proof never persists. Child creation now has its own `TeamSystemChildCreationProof` scope containing the complete child payload and observed parent cursor. The Hub accepts only the canonical `team-child-delegation` source and revalidates it before parent repair, under the parent lock, after register policy, and after child-stream opening. Shipped product composition registers no such source until an owner implements parent-task delegation, so raw child creation fails closed; no Session header, product request body, parent Team, parent task, or hierarchy projection is treated as a creator.

Terminal Team archive is proof-only too. `team-run` retains no durable credential: after its own successful completion or cancellation it holds only one local owner keyed by Team id, mints a fresh `TeamSystemArchiveProof` for each requested cursor, and clears owners and outstanding proofs on close or disposal. The Hub accepts only the `team-run` source's exact `{ kind: 'team-run-terminal-archive', teamId, expectedCursor }` scope, re-resolves it before Team load, under the Team lock including an already-archived idempotent read, and after close policy before `team/archived` append. Archive never persists a proof, owner, or new record field; a Team id, terminal snapshot, `createdBy`, roster, detached SDK server, new TeamRun, or restart cannot restore authority.

Channel summary append is proof-only too. `ChannelSummarizeInput` retains only channel, cursor, bounded source range, exact Envelope ids, text, view-policy ref, and retry key; `TeamSystemChannelSummaryProof` binds that complete payload in the canonical `team-channel-summary` source. No shipped composition registers the source until a summary consumer owns it, so raw summary requests fail closed. The Hub resolves the proof before channel load, under the channel lock, and again after its asynchronous source-range read before WAL append. The summary record retains its existing source/business facts only, never a proof or issuer state; a channel id, policy, WAL range, or read projection cannot reconstruct authority.

## Proof command boundary

The proof-only commands are adapter-owned final admission, recipient receipt, delivery claim, task-attempt start claim, heartbeat, and settlement. Final admission's public input contains only channel, retry key, and text; receipt contains only channel, Envelope, and observed-cursor identities; a delivery claim contains only channel and Envelope identities; a task-start claim contains only task, attempt, assignment-revision, channel, and Envelope identities; heartbeat contains only task, attempt, and observed revision; and settlement adds its typed outcome. The local Link and authenticated WebSocket listener mint a private activation proof after verifying their binding, retain its lease privately, and pass the proof to the Hub. Under the Team/channel locks, the Hub revalidates the binding and derives the sender for final admission, the current recipient for a receipt, the recipient and delivery intent for a delivery claim, and the Team, Participant, activation, and Session for task-start admission. `team-run` registers a distinct source-scoped system proof that can receipt only the current local run's active two-member direct-v3 coordinator final addressed solely to its human. For heartbeat and settlement, the Hub selects the Team from the proof, then revalidates the binding inside the Team lock and derives the exact current lease owner before policy or durable mutation. Ordinary `postChannelEnvelope()` accepts a runtime actor plus JSON-only cursor, retry, and draft fields. Links derive the sender from their activation proof; `team-run` derives only its current human-to-coordinator direct-v3 input; and `team-scheduler-dag` derives only a current task-assignment or participant-review request. The Hub requires the scheduler proof for every task-assignment channel post and consult review request, so an activation actor cannot occupy a scheduler-owned Envelope. A live reviewer resolves a task only through its activation proof and JSON-only task/revision/decision fields; a separate scheduler task-review proof repairs only one exact closed consult response after the Hub validates the task and both Envelope records. Closure, goal, workspace, artifact, task, and maintenance commands need their own source-scoped issuers. A proof forged through a cast, JSON round trip, clone, cross-Team reuse, revoked issuer lease, or stale activation rejects before a policy waterfall or append.

Closure now accepts JSON-only command fields plus `TeamClosureAuthority`: an activation proof derives a current active participant, while `team-run` resolves only exact completion, cancellation, and creation-failure scopes. The Hub routes by the proof's Team, re-resolves it under the Team and attached-channel locks before replay or policy, and journals only the derived durable actor.

Goal update and phase-transition commands now use the canonical JSON-only input plus `TeamActorProof` form. TeamRun issues a private short-lived proof only after its exact coordinator capability and human direct-v3 turn checks; the Hub re-resolves that proof under the Team lock and derives its policy participant. The former raw `participantId` and structural activation-actor forms are removed, and `/goal` remains status-only.

Generic Team lifecycle transition now uses a separate `TeamSystemPhaseProof`. The scheduler can prove only one active-to-stalled transition with its exact unassignable-work reason, while TeamRun can prove one stalled-to-active resume or one human-receipted final from `active` to `quiescing`. The finalization scope binds the Team cursor, final channel and Envelope, and default human/coordinator topology; the Hub locks that channel, rejects unfinished non-channel work, and re-resolves the proof after close policy before its phase append. Fixture state is seeded privately rather than reopening a generic transition command.

Terminal stream compaction now uses `TeamSystemMaintenanceProof`. The scheduler source binds one Team journal or channel WAL, its observed cursor, and its exact `throughSequence`; the Hub resolves that proof before close policy, audit repair, checkpoints, or destructive storage work. Raw system names remain durable attribution only.

Soft interrupts now derive both sides of the command. ACP asks TeamRun for a one-shot proof that names only the current default human-to-coordinator topology; Link discovery and acknowledgement use the target activation's private `TeamActorProof`. The Hub resolves each proof under its Team/channel lock before interrupt policy or a journal append.

Usage recording now uses the activation-proof boundary. `team-agent-client` retains one private lease for each exact current binding and sends only JSON provider/model facts; the Hub revalidates the binding before parent-charge repair, duplicate replay, policy, or append, then derives Team, Participant, Session, and timestamp. Parent-charge propagation remains internal Hub settlement and carries the already-derived durable provenance.

Activation lifecycle commands now use `TeamSystemActivationProof`. `team-activation-controller` retains one exact bind, status, fence, or quiesce scope only for its Hub call; `team-activation-recovery` retains only a wake-cleanup retry for an offline epoch recorded as locally quiesced. The Hub resolves the proof before Team selection and under its lock before policy, lease cleanup, or append, and neither source persists a proof.

Host approval and question actions now use `TeamSystemHumanActionProof`. The API proxy retains a verified pending interaction only after its Hub admission, then issues a fresh cursor-bound terminal proof for each resolution retry. The Hub derives the action, policy actor, outcome, and timestamps under its Team lock; a proxy restart loses the verified entry and logs rather than rebuilding raw authority.

Scheduler assignment and expiry now use `TeamSystemTaskLeaseProof`. `team-scheduler-dag` retains one exact task/participant/activation/wake-channel assignment scope or one elapsed attempt scope only for its Hub call. The Hub resolves it before parent-charge repair and again under the Team and attached wake-channel locks; an assignment rejection closes the just-opened wake channel rather than stranding it.

Scheduler review and wake channel lifecycle now uses `TeamSystemSchedulerChannelProof`. The scheduler scopes one completed review attempt and idle reviewer binding, one pending task and idle assignee activation, or one failed-assignment wake channel; the Hub derives fixed manifests, revalidates the scope before and after repair, then again after policy before attachment or close. An orphan wake may be retired after unrelated Team changes, but a current task lease always protects its wake channel.

Only `expireSchedulerChannelDeliveries()` uses the scheduler TTL delivery-expiry proof family. Its exact scope binds the active Team, attached channel, both observed cursors, the scheduler's clock observation, and the bounded batch limit. The Hub retains the policy-free provider-drive behavior, revalidates the scope immediately before its channel-WAL append, and permits an attached terminal channel to drain pending deliveries.

Coordinator-authored task creation and workflow-plan admission now use `TeamActorProof`. Their JSON fields retain a required retry key or plan facts without raw creator/actor identity; the Hub resolves a current active coordinator before repair and under its Team lock, then derives durable task creator or plan actor. Every newly written Task snapshot retains that creator command, and journal or checkpoint parsing rejects missing provenance. Workflow channel, task-binding, and phase commands remain a separate compiler authority concern.

Direct task-attempt start now uses the current lease owner's `TeamActorProof` and accepts only task/revision/attempt fields; the Hub derives Team, Participant, and activation under its lock. Lease-free task details edits and tombstones likewise require a current coordinator proof. All three revalidate proof and task fence after `task-mutate` policy before append; no request carries a caller-selected owner or activation.

Workflow compilation now uses `TeamSystemWorkflowProof`. TeamRun scopes a compiling plan's channel manifest, channel/task binding, phase payload, or orphan cleanup to one call; the Hub re-resolves it under Team/channel locks. An orphan cleanup proof closes only an attached active workflow channel before the plan binds it, never a bound or non-compiling plan channel.

Current-coordinator default-worker owner proposal and cancellation require `TeamSystemTaskControlProof`. TeamRun scopes the durable creator, task revision and selected payload; the Hub verifies that creator and revalidates the proof after policy before append. Owner proposals require a pending nonworkflow task. The [single-task cancellation decision](../../implemented/architecture/2026-09-06-exact-single-task-cancellation.md) owns cancellation admission and exact-work settlement. Post-release cleanup retains its separate authority lifetime; `cancelTask()` has no actor-free path.

Participant topology now uses `TeamSystemTopologyProof`. Before it publishes a Run, TeamRun may issue one bootstrap participant invitation, membership transition, or initial direct-v3 channel-open scope; after publication it may issue only a declared worker activation or reviewer invitation/transition scope. The Hub resolves the proof before participant policy and under the Team serializer before append or bootstrap WAL creation, so raw participant mutations fail closed while scheduler-owned structural channel opens retain their separate form.

Generic non-workflow channel opens and ordinary closes use `TeamSystemChannelLifecycleProof`. Its runtime-only `channel-lifecycle` dispatch marker selects the generic open route but does not authorize it; the canonical source binds the complete payload and the Hub re-resolves it after policy and after WAL creation before attachment. Shipped product composition registers no generic lifecycle source.

Post-release cleanup uses separate cancellation and finalization proofs. `TeamSystemCancellationCleanupProof` binds one accepted cancellation identity, Team cursor, and exact pending task or active channel; the Hub preflights it before parent-charge repair and resolves it again before policy or append. `TeamSystemFinalizationCleanupProof` binds one human-receipted coordinator final, default topology, Team cursor, and active attached channel; it closes only that channel with a completion-specific reason. Neither proof grants ordinary close, workflow cleanup, or coordinator task-control authority.

## Alternatives considered

**Wrap `ParticipantId` in a new TypeScript type.** Rejected because a caller can still construct the value and the Hub still has no evidence of possession.

**Use the unique active human Participant as every product caller.** Rejected because roster lookup is not authentication and permits any connected Host or SDK client to impersonate that human.

**Persist a capability with the Team.** Rejected because durable logs must retain attribution and business facts, not reusable caller secrets; Link credentials and Host principals have distinct revocation lifetimes.

**Keep unsafe routes while adding proof only to new APIs.** Rejected because every surviving raw write remains a bypass around the new control plane.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every Team write resolves one opaque proof inside the Hub before policy or durable acceptance.
- Link, TeamRun, scheduler, controller, recovery, and retention proofs are issuer- and hook-scoped; expired or cross-Team proofs reject.
- Receipt admission derives its recipient from an activation proof, or from `team-run`'s exact final-receipt proof after the Hub verifies the active default topology and final Envelope.
- Ordinary Envelope admission accepts no caller-selected sender, activation, or Session; scheduler-owned assignment and review drafts reject activation proofs. A reviewer derives authority from its activation proof, while scheduler recovery derives it only from one scoped durable consult response.
- Host/SDK generic writes without an authenticated product principal fail explicitly, while current-run TeamRun operations remain scoped to their owning process; wire schemas contain no actor proof or caller-selected actor fields.
- Closure and audit records retain only Hub-derived durable attribution.
- Local, WebSocket, TeamRun, and system operations retain their valid behavior through proof-based tests on JSON and SQLite; forged and stale proofs fail before mutation.

## Risks

This is a cross-package pre-release contract change touching core schemas, Hub, links, product consumers, Host, SDKs, catalogs, snapshots, and Python projections. Re-enabling a temporarily unavailable management route before its principal issuer exists would recreate the bypass; every reintroduction must include a source-specific proof and denial tests.
