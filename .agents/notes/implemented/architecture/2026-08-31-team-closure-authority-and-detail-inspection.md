# Agent Note: Team closure authority and detail inspection

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-08-31-team-closure-authority-and-detail-inspection.zh.md)

## Problem

Team completion and cancellation could be represented by an ordinary phase write, while task, workspace, review, and product consumers each carried part of the proof needed to decide whether a Team was actually finished. The browser page also exposed a Team summary without an owner-routed way to inspect authoritative channel records, audit entries, task outcomes, or Participant Session descendants.

## Decision

`TeamRuntime` exposes typed `completeTeam()`, `failTeam()`, and `cancelTeam()` commands. Completion requires an exact coordinator-to-human final Envelope, a durable human receipt, a completed objective, and quiescent tasks, activations, human actions, channels, and deliveries. Failure requires terminal task and activation state. Cancellation first appends a `team/cancellation` request, moves active or stalled admission to `quiescing`, cancels lease-free work and pending human actions, and closes channels; active leases or activations remain visible until an owner settles or fences them. A retry with the same key finishes the request once those resources quiesce, while a different key conflicts. The local `TeamRun` Consumer requests cancellation before releasing its coordinator lease; a rejection without an observable cancellation request retains local run ownership for retry. Product Host routes no generic `team.phase` mutation.

The mounted `team-closure-driver` Consumer resumes accepted completion, failure, and cancellation intents from durable Team state after restart. TeamRun persists an independent final-result admission after completion intent and before receipt or local teardown. The driver recovers an intent-only result by appending that same closed-sink admission, returns the resulting cursor, and repairs the receipt only with fresh continuation authority. It also records a coordinator missing-final, structured turn failure, or frozen budget exhaustion before that observation is returned to its caller. Closure-owned pending deliveries receive an explicit `channel/delivery-expired` reason before channel and Team terminal records are committed; an unconfirmed remote termination remains stalled rather than being reported as settled.

The `team-run-result` sink records the exact Team, channel, Envelope, WAL sequence, canonical content fingerprint, derived recipient and durable owner, and Team-scoped idempotency key. Its journal fact is independent of closure intent and receipt; the checkpoint retains that fact and channel retention preserves its referenced content. It remains the closed result owner for unattended system-owned runs. Principal-owned finals instead use `principal-inbox`: the Hub awaits the [human inbox Consumer](../../../../packages/team/team-human-client/README.md) append before recording admission with its exact inbox sequence. Recovery uses the same sink selection and idempotent Envelope identity. Product display acknowledgement is a separate monotonic principal cursor and cannot prove channel delivery. The completed result cannot be reconstructed from a notification or model claim.

Terminal recovery validates every attached channel's Team identity, terminal phase, and settled deliveries against its WAL. A completed Team also requires a matching closure selection and independent final admission, the exact retained Envelope and content fingerprint, and its human receipt. These checks run before a checkpoint or replayed projection is exposed; inconsistent checkpoints fall back to the source journal, while storage and implementation-loading failures remain visible. Final lookups start at the retained WAL cursor so legitimate prefix compaction preserves completed recovery.

First result admission and completion intent require a pending human delivery or a committed human receipt. A durable expiry cannot reserve the result, while an elapsed deadline alone does not invalidate delivery. Once the sink accepts a result, scheduler TTL expiry preserves that exact channel, Envelope, and recipient until receipt, including after restart. This selection happens before the expiry batch limit so other due deliveries remain eligible. Explicit cancellation and failure settle deliveries under their own durable intents.

Closure-driver startup callers share one initial recovery sweep. Each Team serializer owns all coalesced observer actions, detaches their input before queueing, and drains remaining actions after a failed pass before reporting accumulated failures. This prevents a failed or duplicate notification from discarding another accepted observation. Disposal closes new admission synchronously, rejects queued observers whose backend pass has not begun, retains each admitted proof until its backend pass settles, and unregisters the exposed driver even when shutdown fails.

The Hub bridge delegates one exact-epoch recovery action to the activation controller before lifecycle settlement. A changed Team cursor ends that pass and triggers another with fresh authority. Accepted completion advances on Team events regardless of its original actor, so a deployment without a polling pulse still continues after result admission or resource recovery; TeamRun does not retain a separate channel-cleanup window.

Controller stall commands distinguish an invalid proof payload from a concurrent Team write. A proof whose cursor differs from the command is rejected as authority-invalid; after the exact intent and epoch remain valid, an advanced durable cursor produces `TEAM_CURSOR_CONFLICT` so the controller can reread and retry within its configured bound.

Direct cancellation and driver cleanup attempt every selected channel branch before reporting storage failures. A single failure retains its original error; multiple failures are aggregated in execution order, and the durable intent remains retryable. Each branch revalidates authority before policy and before append because an earlier awaited policy can revoke the proof. A retry skips channels already closed, and a failed branch prevents terminal Team admission.

Team and Participant authority grants are immutable typed subsets. Child Teams, Participants, and task requests cannot widen operation hooks, workspace modes, relative read/write scopes, or typed resource ceilings. Task admission reserves declared ceilings against the Team budget, and assignment/usage paths enforce runtime wall-time, retry, concurrency, token, turn, and cost limits before another lease or model step is accepted. A typed cost ceiling rejects a sample or child charge whose provider pricing is unknown instead of counting it as zero. Review assignments carry the exact completed result, reviewer, initiator, and review revision; the reviewer derives its CAS fence from that logged source and sends an idempotent response to the initiator before resolving the task.

Usage replay compares the normalized cost representation retained by the journal. Omitted provider cost resolves through the Team's creation-time rate table; unchanged samples add no usage observation or parent charge, while changed token counts or explicit cost replace the prior sample. Policy receives the raw request. A pricing error remains deferred until phase, cursor, and policy checks pass, preserving their denial precedence; replay after restart uses the retained rate table.

Every workspace-sensitive local consumer resolves its root through the Team allocation attached to the Agent scope before falling back to the Session header. Persistent shells discard their cached process when the allocation changes, and local file-reference indexes are rebuilt for a new allocation root. This keeps filesystem, LSP, skills, instructions, references, sandbox policy, and process tools on the same provider-selected execution root.

Rendered channel views must fit the Team's frozen `maxChannelViewBytes` limit after UTF-8 serialization. A valid projection above that limit raises `TEAM_CHANNEL_BACKPRESSURE` with its actual and maximum byte counts; it does not indicate WAL corruption. Rejection leaves delivery pending without changing Team or channel cursors. A stateless projection policy must produce the same view for the same immutable source, so retrying that oversized input cannot reduce its size. Independent source records may produce a view within the bound. Source provenance and post-read activation authority remain independently checked.

The [channel-view capacity example](../../../../examples/headless-agent/tests/channel-view-capacity.snapshot.ts) uses one pure policy over two actual worker review results. The oversized result remains pending after repeated claims, with no Session view or receipt. The independent smaller result reaches the reviewer model through a logged view and a receipt written after Session flush. Both Teams finish through the public cancellation path on JSON and SQLite.

The browser Team owner exposes cancellable bounded reads for the Team audit page and channel WAL, shows task attempt evidence and artifacts, and opens a Participant's descendant Session through the runtime owner. The detail view remains compact and read-only for mutation surfaces; it does not scrape Session transcripts to derive Team state or create a second composer.

The ACP Link bridge retains its more specific ownership in [the ACP activation Team Link note](2026-08-31-acp-team-link-bridge.md): a remote activation uses a Team-resolved proxy Session to flush admitted input and completion facts before the Link receipt. The existing [review-route](2026-08-28-typed-team-task-review-routes.md), [workspace-root](2026-08-28-shared-team-workspace-root.md), [portable-consumer](2026-07-28-portable-execution-world-consumers.md), and [Team product owner](2026-08-29-local-team-product-run-owner.md) notes remain active owners for their narrower mechanisms.

The closure controller derives unresolved epochs from the durable Team snapshot rather than its live-handle map. Exact provider fencing records immutable `fencedAt` before allocation cleanup; complete quiescence remains a separate fact after release. This distinction preserves a successful termination across a crash even when the provider cannot prove an already exited process tree a second time. Without a live owner, recoverable descriptor or recorded fence, recovery records an exact activation stall and never fabricates offline status. Workspace release events have an owner without requiring a periodic pulse, and accepted recovery work drains before its runtime proofs are revoked. Failure cleanup cancels unfinished tasks and human actions, fails unfinished workflow plans with the original reason, and preserves settled attempt/review evidence.

Controller activation and recovery calls capture their target identities before awaiting work. Every human recovery authorization must still resolve to that selected Team. A handle that fails publication after durable bind remains owned until disposal records quiescence; a cleanup failure retains its stopping entry for retry and is reported together with the publication failure. Shutdown retains proofs for admitted stopping-epoch cleanup. A delayed Team phase response does not overwrite a newer activation binding, and a released entry cannot authorize another queued cleanup.

The workspace recovery Consumer retries transient durable reads within explicit attempt and delay bounds, independently of provider release and confirmation. Recognized format and permission failures stop immediately. This permits recovery after a missed read without requiring another allocation event, while disposal waits for admitted retries before revoking their proofs. Timer durations are bounded by Node's supported range.

Agent Client workspace mutations use a configurable finite cursor retry bound. Each attempt derives the allocation revision and Team cursor from one fresh snapshot and issues new exact authority; provider cleanup is outside the retry loop. A failed delivery close retains the Agent's workspace lease, proof source, unavailable marker, and rejecting pre-step listener until admitted work and mapped resources settle. Core can therefore retry the same owner before activation disposal. Successful local physical release is remembered by that live owner so a later confirmation retry performs no second release; a cold owner still requires provider reconciliation. Team errors, including policy denials, are reported directly instead of automatically retrying confirmation through reconciliation.

Channel creation and cold-recovery failures close an opened WAL before releasing its retained adapter, view-policy, and runtime leases. Every cleanup is attempted; failures retain the original error first and each cleanup error once. An append that committed before reporting failure retains its unattached source records without publishing a channel or attaching it to the Team.

An audit append conflict retires the stale derived-stream handle before a later read reopens it. If that handle's close fails, repair reports the original conflict followed by the cleanup error, each once; a successful close preserves the original conflict unchanged. Business source records and cursors remain authoritative throughout audit repair.

## Alternatives considered

**Infer a human receipt directly from completion intent.** Rejected because intent selects a result but does not prove sink persistence. Recovery must perform the independent sink append before it can acknowledge delivery.

**Keep cancellation as a terminal phase command after callers release every resource.** Rejected because a live or remote owner can outlast the caller's process; without a durable cancellation request, a restart cannot distinguish an intentional admission cutoff from an ordinary quiescing Team or reliably resume cleanup.

**Use one broad grant object or prompt instructions as the authority boundary.** Rejected because a broad JSON capability is difficult to subset and prompt compliance cannot protect channel, workspace, task, or budget mutation. Typed operation, mode, scope, and budget subsets are checked before policy and durable acceptance.

**Let the browser detail page reconstruct Team state from Participant Session transcripts.** Rejected because Sessions cannot authoritatively establish Team cursor, channel order, receipt state, task revision, or audit provenance. The page reads the Team API and opens a Session only as a selected descendant.

**Keep review revision in a model-supplied argument.** Rejected because a reviewer can copy a stale or unrelated revision. The logged assignment source owns the revision and response causation, while the model supplies only decision and reason.

## Consequences

Typed closure facts make completion, failure, and cancellation restart-visible and idempotent, but a remote cancellation whose provider cannot prove termination remains quiescing or stalled rather than being reported as cancelled. The closure driver also persists missing-final, structured turn-failure, frozen-budget, and orphaned-quiescing stalls without a process-local `TeamRun` credential. A local cancellation cannot dispose its only coordinator before the Hub records intent; it re-reads and retries the same typed command when an unrelated accepted record advances the observed cursor, and a concurrent request that already recorded cancellation permits cleanup to continue. Generic phase remains a trusted provider primitive for internal transitional work, while shipped product callers use typed commands.

The Team state and API payloads are wider, and the current Team journal/checkpoint formats are 26 and 27. Child usage is now charged through the same durable subtree aggregate, with pending outbound charges acting as a work admission fence. Durable declarative workflow plans now add whole-plan revisions and task/channel bindings; the detail page performs bounded reads and keeps mutation controls in authorized Host Consumers. Advisory task owner proposals now retain a revision-fenced scheduler hint without transferring authority. Authenticated product-principal binding now covers participant invitation, artifact byte reads, task editing, detached archive/resume, and real-browser/SDK credential admission; full remote recovery remains separate work.

These changes enforce local authority, root consumption, subtree-wide child-usage charging, and the compiled local `TeamWorkflowPlan` Consumer, but do not provide property/real-model/multi-host/browser/GIF/load evidence for the full [native multi-agent work-system proposal](../../proposed/architecture/2026-08-27-native-multi-agent-work-system.md). Those remain named gaps in that proposal rather than implicit guarantees of this implementation.

## Verification

Audit recovery tests use an independent SQLite connection to commit the exact derived values produced by the Hub before the original handle appends. They verify the real conflict, successful close, failed close, and a later read without duplicated facts. JSON separately enforces its single-owner lock and recovers an append committed before an EIO reply; it does not provide a concurrent-writer CAS scenario.

The [human-question example](../../../../examples/headless-agent/tests/human-question.snapshot.ts) runs the standard worker's actual question tool through UserQuestions and ApiProxyService. JSON and SQLite retain a pending human action whose source id matches the real Mux request, then preserve that identity through public TeamRun cancellation and one resolved event. Session tool-call records and backend reads independently confirm the producer and its durable outcome.

The [workspace release restart snapshot](../../../../examples/headless-agent/tests/workspace-release-restart.snapshot.ts) creates an actual task allocation through TeamRun, scheduler, and Agent Client. It confirms physical provider cleanup before injecting a confirmation-write failure and killing the Host. A new JSON or SQLite Loader reconciles the original release request, retries confirmation without repeating cleanup, and retains an exact unconfirmed-worker stall. The shipped profiles wait for provider initialization through the Loader dependency; the [Loader readiness note](../bug-fix/2026-07-20-config-hot-reload-resilience.md) explains its concurrent-waiter check.

The [priced usage snapshot](../../../../examples/headless-agent/tests/priced-usage.snapshot.ts) lets a real coordinator model step emit usage while an actual read-only tool holds the turn open. Agent Client records the sample before its actual binding replays old and fresh cursors. JSON and SQLite retain one usage observation and unchanged derived cost; independent source and JSONL Session reads verify provenance after real TeamRun cancellation.

The [channel opening failure snapshot](../../../../examples/headless-agent/tests/channel-open-failure.snapshot.ts) drives real TeamRun creation through JSON and SQLite failures before and after a source append commits. It holds physical close while verifying retained implementation leases, then confirms ordered original/close/runtime-release errors, zero publication or attachment, and preservation of the committed orphan WAL.

The [assembled final-lifecycle snapshot](../../../../examples/headless-agent/tests/final-lifecycle.snapshot.ts) posts a TTL final through a real local Link, waits for the scheduler's durable expiry, and proves rejection leaves no completion intent, sink admission, or human receipt. A replacement final then completes through TeamRun and the closure driver without a closure polling pulse. Post-commit cursors establish intent, sink, and receipt order; an independent SQLite read after process exit verifies the source records and offline activation.

The [independent Loader recovery snapshot](../../../../examples/headless-agent/tests/final-restart.snapshot.ts) kills the first process after the durable intent, sink admission, or receipt and launches a second process over the untouched JSON or SQLite medium. The intent window records the exact unconfirmed activation as stalled without inventing quiescence. The sink and receipt windows dispose the real coordinator lease before the crash and recover to completed. A third independent Loader verifies cold reads and that recovery adds no duplicate result or receipt. A separate copy supplies crash evidence so test inspection cannot reclaim the original JSON lock or checkpoint its SQLite WAL before recovery.

The [failure and cancellation restart snapshot](../../../../examples/headless-agent/tests/terminal-restart.snapshot.ts) kills the Host after a real failed coordinator turn or TeamRun cancellation commits intent, or after its owner records activation quiescence. A fresh JSON or SQLite Loader retains the active objective and exact epoch, stalls when termination is unconfirmed, and reaches failed or cancelled when quiescence is durable. An unread human message expires under the corresponding closure reason. Recovery sends no model request and creates no final admission or human receipt.

Direct Hub admission tests on JSON and SQLite reject a final while its objective is paused or blocked, preserve the Team cursor and absent receipt, and accept the same result and key after the objective resumes. This checks the sink's own eligibility enforcement independently of TeamRun's caller checks.

Core schema, Hub fold, JSON/SQLite lifecycle, closure-driver restart scan, missing-final, structured-failure, budget-stall, orphan-quiescing, and pending-delivery-expiry tests cover the changed acceptance paths. TeamRun tests gate cancellation admission before local lease disposal, retry an intervening cursor advance without releasing ownership, preserve ownership after an unaccepted intent, record a current turn failure before returning it, and confirm no generic phase transition enters that product path. The Team detail UI suite also exercises stalled/cancelled controls, durable human-attention rows, current and missing Participant bindings, channel/audit/artifact retry states, bounded text previews, and downloads. The assembled keyless Headless Team snapshots cover the worker activation/task-create cursor race and the complete workflow-plan start/wait path. Host and SDK route tests confirm the generic product phase method is unavailable. TypeScript host/client builds pass, the persistence and Cordis catalogs are regenerated, and the remaining release-tier browser/GIF, keyed real-model, distributed, property, and load suites remain explicitly pending.

The principal inbox passes JSON/SQLite restart, duplicate, pagination, visibility and display-cursor tests. Host fetch coverage completes a real Team while its final remains unread, then reads and acknowledges that final after a complete Host restart without a local TeamRun owner. The TypeScript SDK final snapshot calls inbox read and display acknowledgement through the real Loader and stdio runtime. Ordinary human messages, durable action continuations and inbox retention remain separate work.
