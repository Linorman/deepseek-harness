# Agent Note: Local Team Hub durable authority

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-08-27-local-team-hub-durable-authority.zh.md)

## Problem

The [durable storage logs and Team service definition](2026-08-27-durable-storage-logs-and-team-service-definition.md) provides a persistence operation and abstract Team vocabulary, but neither creates an authoritative Team nor replays Team state. Keeping that authority in the experimental Lead Session preserves the identity, recovery, and direct-child coupling the native work-system design removes.

## Decision

`@clocky/clocky-team-hub` implements `TeamRuntime` as an explicitly mounted local `ctx.teams` provider. It opens `team/<TeamId>` journals and `channel/<ChannelId>` WALs only through `ctx.storageLog`, mints independent Team-domain identities, and discovers materialized Team streams through the facility's route-aware listing.

The Hub folds validated Team journals and channel WALs into per-resource projections. It commits Team/participant lifecycle changes, complete task snapshots with activation-authorized create commands and fenced attempt leases, activation bindings, human-action records, provider usage samples/aggregates, and channel attachment through expected cursors; it commits channel opening, closure, authenticated Envelope admission, and recipient receipts in the WAL. Activation bindings preserve every offline epoch by ActivationId, require one Session per local Participant, and reject concurrent resident epochs. Task commands retain the exact creator binding and retry key; journal and checkpoint replay validate their relation and uniqueness. Task attempts retain owner capability eligibility and an agent epoch where applicable; journal and checkpoint replay validate the same lease, deadline, and history rules as public commands. Envelope admission requires an active Team and channel sender, validates explicit active audiences, runs the `send` policy and frozen adapter, then atomically appends the stamped Envelope with adapter follow-up records. The fold derives pending recipient admissions from each adapter delivery plan; a receipt removes one pending admission and maintains a recipient high-water cursor, while a duplicate receipt returns its committed record without another append. A root Team snapshots its configured maximum child depth. A child names both its active parent Team and a non-deleted parent task; the Hub holds that parent's serial queue through child-journal creation, derives the child depth, and persists the inherited cap. A channel is visible only after its Team journal attaches the channel id, so an interrupted unreferenced WAL is not treated as Team state. Durable acceptance precedes projection publication and immutable post-commit observation.

The first `team/created` record carries a revision-one Team goal; later whole `goal/changed` records preserve its compare-and-set lineage, phase, blocked-only explanation, and goal-specific budgets. The Hub authorizes public goal definition and phase mutations through `goal-mutate`; Team resource budgets remain separate creation-time facts. Host approval/question adapters use cursor-fenced `human-action/changed` records, and `recordUsage()` replaces duplicate turn/step samples while stalling a Team after a frozen turn/output-token/cost ceiling is crossed. The [durable Team goal decision](2026-08-28-durable-team-goal.md) owns this objective-state design.

Recovered projections start from a checkpoint only when its serialization, identity, goal/task-create/task-attempt/human-action/usage semantics, pending-delivery ordering, quiescence source, and durable watermark agree; otherwise recovery replays the journal or WAL. Team journal format 26 and Team checkpoint format 27 are independent of channel WAL format 5 and channel checkpoint format 8. A quiescence proof records whether a fencer stopped the old process or a local owner settled it; only the former is cold-replacement eligible. Per-resource serial queues and durable expected-tail checks reject stale commands. Cursor watches register under the same queue that observes the current cursor, and Hub disposal closes admission, settles accepted work within configured bounds, attempts final checkpoints, and closes every owned stream.

Completion intent retains a final only while a durable human receipt or exact pending delivery still exists. A previously expired, unreceipted final cannot reserve closure authority, because neither TeamRun nor recovery can acknowledge a delivery whose expiry is already durable. The clock crossing an Envelope deadline does not by itself expire the delivery.

Journal and checkpoint reconstruction apply the same Team-to-Participant grant containment check when both grants exist. Operations, workspace modes, read/write scope prefixes and supplied budget ceilings cannot widen the Team grant; omitted optional grants remain readable. Scope prefixes match complete path segments, so `packages/team/review` is contained by `packages/team`, while `packages/teammate` is not. Journal and checkpoint task owner proposals must name a retained Team participant. Leaving the Team preserves that identity, so historical proposals do not require an active participant. Checkpoint validation follows references in both directions: workflow tasks must name their plan and template, while every plan binding must name the corresponding retained task and an attached channel when present. Partial compiling state remains valid. Final admission timestamps must lie between Team creation and the checkpoint cursor's update time. Rejecting these inconsistencies preserves journal truth instead of publishing corrupted checkpoint facts.

Workflow revisions retain every admitted template-to-task binding and, once present, the channel binding. Adding a channel cannot conceal dropped or retargeted task bindings; terminal revisions cannot remove or replace the channel. These checks apply when replaying a journal or a suffix after a valid checkpoint and preserve incremental compiling states. The serialized formats are unchanged because the rejected revisions violate existing binding ownership.

Core schemas own cancellation/closure compatibility, participant kind/owner compatibility, required task creator and workflow identities, and workflow definition/internal binding validity. Workflow result schemas require unique template ids and inclusion of every selected template; the fold retains the exact result count, which completes set equality. The sole typed result writer, `transitionWorkflowPlan`, validates the selected template order and count before committing its cloned result. Journal and checkpoint parsers apply those schemas before folding; local Hub constructors derive these fields from validated command inputs and admitted state. The fold preserves cross-resource and cross-revision checks, including ready-plan channel presence, without repeating those parser rules. The fold checks workflow identity uniqueness once before building its map and allocation update time once before validating its transition. Non-cancellation closure cleanup receives only records admitted by the closure whitelist; its resource-specific checks still reject new or reopened work. These shared prerequisites avoid repeating checks whose inputs cannot change between the validating caller and callee. The synchronous cancellation whitelist rejects final-admission and goal-change records before event dispatch; their individual cases do not repeat the already-enforced cancellation test.

Human-action admission closes when Team cancellation is admitted, and completion/failure cleanup also rejects new or reopened requests. A checkpoint during quiescing may retain an earlier pending request so its owner can resolve or cancel it late. Every terminal Team projection requires terminal tasks and workflow plans, released workspace allocations, an empty pending parent-usage ledger, offline activations with durable `quiescedAt` facts, and settled human actions. The same check runs after every journal record and after checkpoint reconstruction, so records following a terminal marker cannot reopen work. Quiescing and stalled projections may retain resources awaiting cleanup. Channel phase and delivery facts belong to separate WAL streams and remain the Hub's cross-stream validation responsibility.

Workflow result content retains its task-attempt provenance: each completed result must match the task's last completed outcome, and any supplied failure detail must match its last failed outcome. Omitting optional failure detail remains valid. Both journal replay and checkpoint reconstruction enforce this relation, so matching task/template ids and phases cannot conceal rewritten summaries, artifact references, or failure messages.

Current task leases must own distinct wake channels. Checkpoint reconstruction rejects duplicate task identities before building the task map, so a set of observed channel ids is sufficient to validate wake uniqueness across its single traversal. Journal assignment requires attached wake channels, subsequent lease and history records preserve those identities, and attachments are retained. Checkpoint reconstruction checks every retained attempt's attachment before validating quiescence. The quiescence proof then checks its exact wake set against the validated attempts settled at its timestamp without repeating attachment checks; outcome and active-lease checks remain independent.

The checkpoint schema permits omitted human-action, usage-sample, child-charge, pending-parent-charge, and workflow-plan collections; reconstruction materializes empty maps. Omitted usage becomes a zero aggregate. These fields remain optional in the durable schema. A quiesced activation has a separate parser rule: when `quiescedAt` is present, its complete wake-channel list is required.

A checkpointed activation's `quiescedAt` must lie between its Team's `createdAt` and `updatedAt`. Monotonic journal timestamps and equality between a new proof's time and its record establish the same relation during replay. Checkpoint reconstruction rejects an out-of-range proof before accepting the activation and falls back to the journal. Core binding refinements and the Hub's complete proof constructor already require offline status and a trusted source; the fold retains record-time equality and admitted-proof immutability. Initial bindings still have a dedicated proof rejection before the general initial lifecycle transition, preserving the precise admission diagnostic.

A checkpointed activation's `fencedAt` must lie between Team creation and the checkpoint update, and cannot precede the accepted closure or cancellation request. Cancellation uses `cancellation.requestedAt`, because its terminal cancel closure is written after resource cleanup and may follow the fence. Journal record monotonicity and the fence constructor's record-time equality establish the same lower bound during replay. The checkpoint check preserves these recorded relationships without treating the recovery descriptor as independent proof of process termination.

Root Teams retain an empty outbound parent-charge ledger. Creation starts with an empty map, parent-charge admission requires a parent Team, nested child-charge folding adds the next-hop entry only when that parent exists, and settlement only removes entries. Checkpoint reconstruction rejects every root pending entry before constructing the ledger. Team lineage remains immutable through journal replay, so incoming child charges use these established ownership rules without repeating a root-ledger check.

## Alternatives considered

**Retain the experimental Lead Session as the Team journal.** Rejected because a durable Team would still depend on one live Agent/Session identity and inherit direct-child recovery semantics.

**Store Team and channel state only in process memory.** Rejected because restart, HMR, and independently opened local Hubs would lose or disagree about accepted Team state.

**Expose delivery, Agent runtime placement, or product APIs from the Hub.** Rejected because those roles require their own durable ownership and authorization rules. The local provider owns durable admission and receipts but not Agent delivery or remote transport; the [native multi-agent work-system proposal](../../proposed/architecture/2026-08-27-native-multi-agent-work-system.md) retains remote replay, scheduler, workspace, and product work.

## Consequences

Sender-scoped post retry keys are bounded per channel; a new key at capacity is rejected without retiring an accepted retry.

The local Hub can create, recover, list, watch, and transition Team state without a Lead Session. It covers bounded root-or-child Team hierarchy, revisioned Team goals, participant membership, durable activation epochs, fenced task attempts and dependency validation, channel manifests, authenticated Envelope admission, derived pending recipient admissions, durable receipts, channel WAL reads, exact visible artifact-reference lookup, closure, and cursor watches. It does not create or own participant handles, select task owners, allocate workspaces, or provide default product entry points.

Pending delivery checkpoints retain source order per recipient and reject a reordered projection. Page reads therefore scan the retained order without sorting every current pending delivery.

The provider depends on `clocky-team` and `clocky-storage-log`, never on AgentLoop or experimental Team code. Headless and Web mount it through SQLite-routed logs at `$CLOCKY_HOME/team-storage.sqlite`; JSON log storage remains an explicit development, test, or custom-composition backend. Future providers can implement the same `TeamRuntime` operations over another persistence or placement model.

The Phase 1 decision remains active because the log contract, identity separation, and storage ownership continue to constrain this provider. The [durable Team goal decision](2026-08-28-durable-team-goal.md) owns objective state; the [durable task attempt lease decision](2026-08-28-durable-task-attempt-leases.md) owns task-specific attempt/fence design. The native work-system proposal remains active because its later phases are not shipped. The [implicit-Lead Team retirement decision](../simplification/2026-08-29-retire-implicit-lead-agent-teams.md) owns the removed distinct implementation.

## Verification

The Hub tests compose it with real JSON and SQLite log backends, restart each backend, and reconstruct Team, nested hierarchy, Goal revision, roster, activation epochs, task attempts, and attached-channel projections. Shipped Headless and Web composition tests prove their Team logs select the shared SQLite path while Web domain data remains on JSON. They exercise depth-cap, missing/deleted-task, inactive-parent, arithmetic-overflow, stale-goal-revision, goal policy, invalid-goal journal/checkpoint, and invalid-checkpoint fallback; activation identity/Session/residency rejection; task dependency/cycle and attempt fence rejection; task revision conflicts; ordered WAL reads; cursor watches; Envelope cursor, membership, policy, adapter, size, atomic follow-up, observer, and restart behavior; and receipt idempotence, high-water ordering, pending-intent validation, and checkpoint restoration. The invariant companion rejects post-commit Team, Goal, or identified channel notifications that lack the Hub's authoritative loaded projection.

Storage-backed regressions cover JSON and SQLite checkpoint corruption of final lifetime, Team/recipient/owner/closure provenance, and workflow channel/task/template references; each rejected checkpoint recovers the original journal facts. Separate tests reject final content proofs that disagree with retained WAL content and reject an already expired unreceipted final before closure admission, then accept a later final with the unused retry key.

Serialized JSON and SQLite regressions cover closure ordering, immutable child-usage origins, pending parent charges, workspace attempt ownership and provider lifecycle timestamps, channel expiry sources and summary identities, and partial workflow checkpoint recovery. Workflow binding retention is tested with journal-only recovery and a valid checkpoint followed by a corrupt suffix. The invariant companion retains its exhaustive `assertNever`; only the default and helper for the statically closed `TeamEvent` alias are excluded from coverage, with unknown JSON event kinds rejected by `teamEventSchema`.

Additional JSON and SQLite regressions reject missing creator/workflow fields, invalid participant owners, malformed workflow definitions and internal bindings, self-origin child charges, inconsistent usage aggregates, and usage that loses its task attempt or Session. Quiescence tests reject active leases, non-quiescence outcomes, and mismatched historical wakes; valid released attempts remain recoverable. Missing-field unit fixtures cross JSON parsing instead of casting incomplete objects into required task types.

Wake ownership tests reject shared or unattached current lease wakes and recover distinct leases from the journal when a checkpoint has either invalid relation. A released task retains its creator after that activation becomes quiesced and offline, including lease-free edits after a checkpoint. Attempts settled earlier remain in history without entering a later quiescence wake proof. Missing, incomplete, or foreign wake proofs and detached historical wake references trigger checkpoint fallback while preserving the exact quiescence facts and subsequent task edits.

JSON and SQLite tests compact a valid journal for a failed, archived Team before reading checkpoints that omit each optional collection or usage field, individually and together. With the creation prefix removed, successful recovery proves checkpoint acceptance rather than journal fallback. Nonzero usage with both ledgers omitted is verified specifically for these archived Team reads.

Human-action closure regressions use JSON and SQLite journals and checkpoints to reject post-cancellation admission and terminal pending requests, preserve pending requests across intent recovery, accept late resolution/cancellation, and reject reopening. Invalid terminal checkpoints fall back to journals with the original settled request.

Terminal-resource JSON and SQLite tests reject unfinished tasks (including review), resident or unquiesced activations, each unreleased allocation lifecycle, pending parent charges, and compiling/ready workflows. Terminal checkpoints with unresolved resources fall back to the original journal, with a valid rules marker making rejection observable even for the private parent-charge ledger. Completed fixture baselines retain an attached final WAL, independent admission, matching human receipt, and closed channel; pre-admission windows retain only the pending WAL prefix. Valid settled resources and post-intent cleanup remain recoverable. These fixtures exercise durable data relationships; their quiescence records do not independently prove that a process stopped.

Workflow outcome regressions persist assigned, running, and settled task records before projecting the workflow result. JSON and SQLite reject rewritten completed results or explicit failure details in journals and fall back from the same corruption in checkpoints; a projection omitting optional failure detail remains accepted.

Closure replay tests validate a complete failure/quiescing/terminal/archive prefix before checking duplicate archive or post-archive records. JSON and SQLite also reject cross-Team intents and new/reactivated task, allocation, activation, or workflow identities after failure intent, while preserving legitimate preservation, release, task settlement, and quiescence cleanup.

Final/closure phase tests reject duplicate admissions, admission during failure cleanup or after failure, terminal completion before objective completion, and goal changes excluded by the retained closure. Successful completion retains the actual final WAL, independent admission, human receipt, and quiescence facts. Checkpoint fallback preserves valid completed goals when a checkpoint changes their phase or completion intent relation. Partial intents and repeated stalled updates remain recoverable through goal completion and a fresh quiescing transition.

The late failure/cancellation phase tests first recover a serialized, resource-free failed Team without a retained closure, which the current durable reader accepts. That prefix isolates the phase checks: a normal driver-produced terminal prefix already has a closure and reaches duplicate-intent or closure-whitelist checks first. The test establishes durable-input reachability, not an alternate driver completion sequence.

Activation-quiescence JSON and SQLite tests reject individually well-formed initial proof bindings, new proofs with mismatched record timestamps, and mutations or complete removal of an admitted proof. Resident proofs and missing source/wake fields are rejected by the actual journal parser. Corrupt checkpoints with quiescence before Team creation or after their recorded Team update time recover the original valid proof from the journal. Existing wake, outcome, and active-lease checks remain exercised by the durable-workspace suite.

Human task-creation JSON and SQLite replay rejects missing or nonhuman creator references and new tasks attributed to a departed human, while retaining existing tasks' creators through lease-free edits and checkpoint recovery. Distinct task ids cannot share one creator/key pair; damaged checkpoints fall back to a journal with two valid keys. Different human participants can reuse the same key because attribution is part of command identity.

Activation-fence JSON and SQLite regressions recover the journal proof when a checkpoint predates Team creation or its accepted failure/cancellation request. A stopping activation with a live allocation accepts a new fence through the public command and current registered proof source, retaining release-requested allocation state until full quiescence. A checkpoint marker proves that cancellation accepts its fence before the later terminal cancel closure.

Workflow selection regressions distinguish omitted selected templates rejected by the journal parser from additional results rejected by the fold count check. JSON and SQLite retain exact parser/count diagnostics and recover the valid journal after either damaged checkpoint; a separate marker proves acceptance of the valid selected-result checkpoint.

Owner-proposal JSON and SQLite regressions reject an absent participant in the journal and recover the retained journal owner from the same damaged checkpoint. A valid checkpoint retains its marker and a proposal whose participant has left. Summary checkpoint tests retain a real source Envelope and valid covered range, then move only the summary cursor beyond its checkpoint; source validation succeeds, while projection reconstruction rejects the cursor and restores the WAL manifest. A valid manifest marker separately proves checkpoint acceptance.

Participant grant JSON and SQLite regressions accept descendant read/write scopes and reject adjacent textual prefixes in journals. Checkpoints that widen operations, workspace modes, read/write scopes, or budget ceilings recover the journal grant. Valid checkpoint markers prove acceptance of narrowed grants and each permitted omission of Team or Participant grants. These cases exercise the existing Participant admission and durable-reader rules; they do not introduce a child-delegation consumer.

Root-ledger JSON and SQLite regressions accept incoming child usage while rejecting an outbound parent-charge record before a later charge can fold. Corrupt root checkpoints fail at pending-ledger admission and recover the original aggregate; nested checkpoints retain their next-hop charge. Exact diagnostics and checkpoint markers identify which existing ownership check rejects each serialized inconsistency.
