# @clocky/clocky-team-hub

English | [中文](README.zh.md)

`@clocky/clocky-team-hub` is the local authoritative `TeamRuntime` provider at `ctx.teams`. Mount it with `@clocky/clocky-team`, `@clocky/clocky-storage-log`, and a selected log backend. It owns the `team/<TeamId>` journal and `channel/<ChannelId>` WAL streams, including durable declarative workflow plans and their task/channel bindings, and imports neither Agent, Session, AgentLoop, nor the experimental Team packages.

Channel opening atomically appends `opened`, `pending`, and one invitation per manifest member. Members resolve to required unless the generic or authenticated-human open request explicitly marks them optional; structural TeamRun/workflow/scheduler channels require every endpoint. `channelInvitationTimeoutMs` defaults to `30000` and freezes each invitation deadline. The last required acknowledgement and `active` share a WAL batch. Only direct-v4 broadcasts may omit an unacknowledged optional recipient; their exact `deliveryIntents` remain fixed across later acknowledgements. Optional expiry needs `allowParticipantRemoval()` from the retained adapter; unsupported removal fails the channel. Closing first stops admission, ends pending invitations, then writes the terminal record. See [admission](../team-channel-admission/README.md).

## Durable Team state

An ordinary post receives JSON-only cursor/retry/draft fields plus a runtime actor. An activation proof derives its current sender; the `team-human-actor` source derives an authenticated product human sender; the `team-run` source may derive only scoped human input, and the `team-scheduler-dag` source may derive only a current task-assignment or review request. A task-assignment channel and a consult review request reject activation proof posts. A live reviewer resolves a task through its activation proof with no caller-selected Team or Participant field; the scheduler may instead repair an exact closed consult response through its separate source-scoped task-review proof after the Hub validates the full response provenance. An adapter-owned final command receives a runtime `TeamActorProof` with only channel, retry-key, and text fields. A receipt accepts only channel, Envelope, cursor, and a runtime actor, never a caller-selected recipient, activation, or Session: an activation proof derives its current recipient, while a registered `team-run` system proof may acknowledge only its scoped active two-party direct-v3 coordinator final addressed solely to its human. A delivery claim receives an activation proof with only channel and Envelope identities, and a task-attempt start claim receives it with only task, attempt, assignment-revision, channel, and Envelope identities. A task-attempt heartbeat or settlement receives the same proof with only task, attempt, and revision fields; settlement also carries its outcome. The Hub resolves every proof again while holding the appropriate Team/channel queues. It verifies the receipt actor, `team-run` source scope, current topology, and nonempty coordinator final before dispatch policy, pending-delivery lookup, cursor comparison, duplicate receipt replay, or WAL append. It derives the sender for final admission, the recipient for a receipt, the recipient and delivery intent for a delivery claim, and the Team, Participant, activation, and Session for task-start, heartbeat, and settlement admission before adapter, policy, idempotency, or WAL admission. A forged, revoked, replaced, offline, or cross-scope proof rejects with `TEAM_ACTOR_PROOF_INVALID`; a valid proof for another activation cannot perform an owner command.

The Hub owns Team/participant/task/activation state, channel admission, and replay-validated journals. Task mutations use revision and attempt fences; goal mutations revalidate an opaque activation or authenticated-human proof under the same Team queue and derive the policy participant from it. A wake channel is exclusive to one current lease and retained on its settled attempt. A recovery-bearing offline activation needs durable quiescence before replacement: `fenceActivation()` records an externally fenced proof, while `quiesceActivation()` records a locally settled proof; both release leases and retire wake channels. Only the fenced proof permits cold replacement. Team creation snapshots deployment limits into `rules.teamLimits`; participant, activation, task, channel, Envelope, pending-delivery, retry-key, attempt, lease, and model usage operations enforce that immutable snapshot. Terminal Teams can be archived with a durable marker that leaves their journals readable while default listings omit them. The same queue checks protect journal and checkpoint replay. Creation-time `maxRetriesPerTeam` and typed `maxRetries` bound new retries across all task records; the tighter ceiling applies, and each task's first attempt consumes no retry quota. Typed `maxConcurrency` caps new simultaneous task assignments while admitted attempts remain able to finish. `maxWallTimeMsPerTeam` and typed `maxWallTimeMs` reject new channel/task work at or after the tighter creation-time deadline while leaving cancellation and archival available. Host approval/question adapters can append cursor-fenced `human-action/changed` records; settled actions and request details survive Hub restart. `recordUsage()` replaces duplicate turn/step samples and appends a durable token/turn/cost aggregate for the full Team subtree; a child Team commits its sample first, then retries an idempotent parent charge before allowing more child work. A configured provider/model `usageRates` table prices samples that omit a provider cost, while an explicit sample cost remains authoritative. Crossing a frozen ceiling stalls the Team. The Hub records cancellation in two stages: `team/cancellation` closes admission and cancels lease-free work, while active leases and activations remain visible until their owners settle or are fenced. A retry with the same cancellation key continues the durable request and reaches terminal `cancelled` only after those resources and channels quiesce; a different key is rejected.

Root creation requires an exact `team-run` root-creation proof; the Hub revalidates it after policy and stream opening, then stores the derived non-secret system origin as `TeamSnapshot.createdBy`. Child creation requires a separate exact `TeamSystemChildCreationProof` from the canonical `team-child-delegation` source. Its scope binds the complete child payload and an observed parent cursor; the Hub verifies it before parent repair, under the parent queue, after register policy, and after opening the child stream. Shipped composition mounts no child-delegation source until a parent-task consumer owns the operation, so raw child creation fails closed and neither a parent Team/task nor hierarchy projection supplies authority.

The rendered text of a claimed model view must fit the Team's frozen `maxChannelViewBytes` limit in UTF-8, including the Team/channel identities, adapter view and policy projection. Oversized valid output rejects with `TEAM_CHANNEL_BACKPRESSURE` and reports actual and maximum bytes. It leaves the pending delivery and channel cursor unchanged. A versioned view policy is pure: retrying the same immutable source retains the same capacity refusal. An independent source whose projection fits can still be delivered through that policy, subject to source provenance and activation proof checks.

Direct v4 null-audience messages address every other acknowledged manifest member. Admission checks their current active Participant state under the Team/channel locks; each recipient retains an independent pending delivery and receipt. A v4 final requires an exact coordinator/human two-party manifest, an actual coordinator Agent sender and actual human recipient, an explicit singleton audience and turn delivery. Channel role labels alone cannot grant human final authority. The [direct adapter](../team-channel-direct/README.md) owns content and audience protocol rules.

Channel creation and cold recovery retain their acquired adapter, view-policy and adapter-runtime leases until an opened stream has finished closing after failure. Cleanup attempts every release even when stream close or adapter-runtime cleanup fails, and reports the original creation or recovery error together with cleanup errors. Recovery owns stream cleanup from checkpoint reading through WAL replay, including failures before an implementation is acquired; its caller only publishes a successfully recovered projection. No Team attachment or channel notification is published for a failed creation. If an append committed before its result was lost, the unattached WAL remains durable and inaccessible through Team channel reads.

Terminal archive requires JSON-only Team/cursor fields plus either an exact `TeamSystemArchiveProof` from `team-run` or an authenticated-human proof bound to the same cursor. The Hub binds that scope before it loads the Team, re-resolves it under the Team queue even for an idempotent already-archived read, checks a terminal phase and cursor before `close` policy, then re-resolves it immediately before appending `team/archived`. The archive marker keeps its existing shape while the current Team journal/checkpoint version gates replay; the durable Team never retains the proof, terminal owner, or a reusable archive credential.

`recordUsage()` accepts only a current activation proof and JSON-only provider/model facts. The Hub revalidates that binding under the Team queue and derives Team, Participant, Session, and timestamp before parent-charge repair, duplicate replay, policy, or a journal append. Samples without provider cost use the frozen rate table before retry comparison, so identical normalized reports retain their original usage record and parent charge. Usage policy receives the raw input; pricing failures are reported only after policy accepts the mutation.

Typed Team budgets and creation-time deployment ceilings both apply; the effective ceiling is the smaller defined value. Typed token/turn counts admit zero, and typed cost ceilings also admit fractional units. `recordUsage()` retains observed usage before stalling on an overrun, including late accounting received while stalled. Closure-driver scans stall at token, turn, cost, and wall-time equality without requiring another model observation; zero exhausts those ceilings.

An integration task stores the completed source task/attempt, provider, target, expected target revision, and proposal or merge mode in the Team task record. Its source must already be a completed attempt; creation runs `workspace-integrate` policy, and a completed integration attempt must retain a matching target result with a target version for success or conflict paths for a failed merge. The Hub owns this authorization and durable outcome while the workspace provider owns the live Git or artifact operation.

Activation lifecycle commands carry JSON-only cursor/binding/status fields plus `TeamSystemActivationProof`. The Hub accepts controller scopes only for their exact bind, status, fence, or quiesce operation, and accepts recovery only for a retained wake-cleanup retry of an offline locally quiesced epoch. It resolves the proof before selecting the Team and again under that queue, then matches the exact current binding before policy, task-lease cleanup, or a journal append.

Human-action commands carry only Team/cursor fields plus `TeamSystemHumanActionProof`. The Host API proxy may issue an exact pending admission or terminal resolution only while it retains the verified interaction entry; the Hub re-resolves that scope under the Team queue, derives the durable action and policy actor, and never accepts a caller-selected action, participant, Session, or outcome. A closure or cancellation intent, or a terminal Team phase, rejects new pending actions before policy. Matching upsert retries still return the existing action, including a cancelled action; already admitted pending actions may still record their terminal outcomes. Terminal Team journals and checkpoints cannot retain pending human actions. Every terminal Team projection also requires released allocations, terminal tasks and workflow plans, no pending parent charge, and offline activations with quiescence proof. Quiescing or stalled projections may retain work awaiting cleanup; channel phase and delivery settlement are checked against their separate WALs.

`assignTask()` and `expireTaskAttempt()` carry JSON-only task/lease fields plus `TeamSystemTaskLeaseProof`. Only the scheduler source can select one exact assignment or elapsed attempt; the Hub resolves the proof before parent-charge repair or wake-channel work and again under the Team and attached channel queues before task-assign policy or journal acceptance.

`openSchedulerReviewChannel()`, `openSchedulerWakeChannel()`, `closeSchedulerFailedWakeChannel()`, and `expireSchedulerChannelDeliveries()` carry `TeamSystemSchedulerChannelProof`. The Hub derives fixed consult or task-assignment manifests from the selected operation, validates the exact review attempt/reviewer binding or pending task/assignee binding before and after repair, and re-resolves after policy before attaching or closing a WAL. Delivery expiry instead validates the scheduler-owned clock and exact Team/channel cursors under both locks immediately before appending the bounded expiry batch; it is policy-free and permits an attached terminal channel to drain its retained pending deliveries. Failed-wake cleanup accepts an orphan after unrelated Team changes, but rejects any channel still referenced by a current task lease.

Coordinator-authored task creation and workflow-plan admission use `TeamActorProof`. Authenticated product task creation and lease-free task mutations use `TeamHumanActorProof`; the Hub derives the durable creator or reviewer from the active human and never accepts a caller-selected identity. Their JSON inputs contain a task idempotency key or plan facts but no raw creator/actor; the Hub resolves the proof before repair and under the Team queue, requires the active agent role `coordinator`, and derives durable creator provenance before replay, policy, or a journal append.

Workflow compilation uses a separate `TeamSystemWorkflowProof` from TeamRun. The Hub accepts a workflow-plan channel open only with an exact compiling plan revision, current coordinator binding, and full manifest scope, then resolves exact scopes again for channel/task binding and phase changes. A dedicated cleanup proof can close an attached unbound workflow channel after a compiler failure, but rejects a bound or non-compiling plan channel. Journal replay preserves every admitted template-to-task and channel binding across later revisions while allowing incremental compilation.

Default-worker task owner proposals and current-coordinator cancellation use `TeamSystemTaskControlProof`. The Hub derives the coordinator from its exact scope, verifies that the nonworkflow task retains the same durable creator, and rejects a stale run, revision, owner target, or proof before policy or durable mutation. It does not apply this authority to cancellation cleanup after the coordinator has released.

Participant invitations and membership phases use `TeamSystemTopologyProof` for trusted topology sources and `TeamHumanActorProof` for authenticated product calls targeting non-human participants; human participant creation remains topology-only; the Hub compares its exact descriptor, cursor, participant, expected phase, and selected next phase before `invite` or `activate` policy and again while holding the Team queue. `team-run` may use bootstrap scopes before it publishes a Run, then worker or reviewer scopes only for their closed operations. A topology proof can open only the initial active direct-v3 human/coordinator channel with its exact ordered manifest. Other non-workflow opens require a `TeamSystemChannelLifecycleProof` and its runtime-only generic dispatch marker; the Hub binds the complete source scope before policy, re-resolves it after policy, and rechecks it after WAL creation before attachment.

Post-release cleanup is split by durable lifecycle evidence. `TeamSystemCancellationCleanupProof` can cancel one exact pending task or close one exact active channel only while the matching TeamRun cancellation remains current; stale proofs are rejected before parent-charge repair, policy, or journal work. `TeamSystemFinalizationCleanupProof` can close one exact active attached channel only while its default direct-v3 topology retains a human-receipted coordinator final; it revalidates the final, topology, Team cursor, and channel cursor under the Team/channel queues before close policy or WAL append. Neither proof cross-authorizes ordinary close, workflow cleanup, or coordinator task control.

`requestParticipantInterrupt()` accepts only TeamRun's current human-to-coordinator interrupt proof, resolves the exact idle/running coordinator target under the Team/channel queues, and runs `interrupt` policy with the derived human requester. A matching unacknowledged target is idempotent; only the target Link's activation proof may list and acknowledge it, and acknowledgement neither cancels a Team nor settles a turn.

Every accepted mutation updates the in-memory projection only after its log append succeeds, then emits the immutable `team/changed`, `goal/changed`, workflow-plan, or identified `channel/changed` observation. Reads reconcile a suffix appended by another SQLite-backed Hub before returning, while a stale expected-tail mutation still invalidates its local projection instead of silently merging concurrent durable state. Cursor reads and watches expose ordered channel records and Team/channel advancement without a subscribe gap. A separate `audit/<TeamId>` or `audit/<TeamId>/channel/<ChannelId>` projection is repaired from the authoritative stream before `readAudit()` returns; audit append failures are logged and retried without rolling back the business mutation. Policy denials are emitted as immutable Team audit observations without advancing the business cursor, and `TeamRuntime.getMetrics()` exposes active-admission, event, denial, delivery, assignment, retry, compaction, checkpoint, audit-repair, and audit-failure counters for operations.

An activation-bound Link keeps its revocable proof lease private; the Hub receives only its opaque runtime proof, never a proof on a wire or in durable state. Its receipt input is limited to channel, Envelope, and observed cursor; the Hub revalidates the binding in the Team/channel queues and derives the recipient before policy or durable admission. For heartbeat and settlement, caller-controlled input is limited to task, attempt, and observed revision, with a typed settlement outcome. The Hub uses the proof only to find the Team queue, then revalidates the binding inside that lock and derives the exact task-attempt owner before policy or durable mutation. Closure commands likewise parse JSON-only input separately from an opaque authority, route it to the selected Team, and re-resolve it under the Team and attached-channel queues before replay, policy, or append. An activation proof derives an active participant; `team-run` may derive only its exact direct-v3 completion, cancellation, or creation-failure scope. The Hub stores only that derived participant or `{ kind: 'system', name: 'team-run' }` attribution, never a raw proof or caller-selected actor. Phase transitions use a separate opaque system proof: only the scheduler may create an exact active-to-stalled transition, while TeamRun may resume a stalled Team or fence an accepted human-receipted final from `active` to `quiescing`. That fence holds the Team and final-channel queues, validates the exact default topology and final, rejects unfinished non-channel work, and stops later channel admission before local release.

`admitTeamFinalResult()` appends one independent `team/final-admitted` fact under TeamRun's closed proof. The Hub binds the exact Team/channel/Envelope and WAL sequence to a canonical SHA-256 payload fingerprint, derives the human recipient and owner, and retains the Team-scoped idempotency key in its checkpoint. A system final receipt requires this durable acceptance. A completion intent requires its final recipient to be a human Participant with a durable owner. Before the first result admission or completion intent, the exact final must have a durable human receipt or a pending human delivery; a durable expiry without receipt rejects that final and leaves a later final admissible. Elapsed wall time alone does not replace the expiry record. An already accepted result remains idempotent after its delivery settles. TTL expiry preserves its exact human delivery until receipt, including after restart; other due deliveries still consume the bounded expiry batch. Explicit cancellation and failure may settle that delivery through their own expiry records. Recovery of a completion intent first appends any missing admission and returns the new Team cursor; only a later pass with fresh authority may repair the receipt. Completion remains nonterminal until the receipt, owned resources, and channels settle. This closed result sink does not implement a principal inbox or display acknowledgement.

Terminal compaction accepts only a scheduler-owned `TeamSystemMaintenanceProof`, not a caller-selected system name. Its exact scope binds the Team/channel, expected cursor, and source prefix bound; the Hub re-resolves it under the held serializers before close policy, audit repair, checkpoint writes, or compaction. The close-policy facts carry the derived `{ kind: 'system', name: 'team-scheduler-dag' }` actor while durable prefix state never retains the proof.

Failure continuation cancels pending and review tasks without changing their attempt or review history, cancels pending human actions with a `team-failed` outcome, and fails unfinished workflow plans with the original Team reason. Quiescence under a failure or cancellation intent cancels released task attempts instead of reopening retryable work. Provider-confirmed termination can persist immutable `fencedAt` together with one allocation release request; offline/quiescent admission still waits for every allocation to be released. A resource stall retains its exact intent, allowing later closure recovery to finish it. Cancellation requires every activation to be offline with durable quiescence proof before channel cleanup or terminal admission, including epochs recovered with offline status alone. `cancelTeam()` and closure-driver continuations attempt every selected channel cleanup in order and report independent failures together after all branches settle. Each delivery-expiry or channel-close branch revalidates its closure proof before policy and again before append; a retry uses the same durable intent and skips channels already closed.

## Recovery and configuration

The Host and SDK management surfaces call `listTeamsPage()`, `listParticipantsPage()`, `listTasksPage()`, and `readChannelPage()` so one response never materializes an unbounded task, roster, Team, or channel-WAL page. Each method uses a caller cursor and positive limit, returns an optional continuation only when a look-ahead proves another item exists, and applies `recoveryPageSize` as the provider cap. `expireSchedulerChannelDeliveries()` accepts only an exact scheduler channel proof and appends per-recipient TTL expiry records for deliveries whose folded deadline has passed. `readChannelSummarySource()` and `summarizeChannel()` require a current channel-member coordinator or payload-bound authenticated human. The [explicit summary Consumer](../team-channel-summary/README.md) owns the canonical output proof. The Hub checks the committed range, exact source ids and canonical SHA-256 fingerprint, channel-wide visibility, immutable policy, cursor and current admission before append. WAL/checkpoint recovery checks provenance against source Envelopes; summary ranges remain pinned during compaction. Matching retries return the original durable result. `compactChannel()` and `compactTeam()` are trusted retention operations for terminal channels and Teams: each requires an authorized actor, a current audit projection, and a checkpoint; channel compaction additionally requires zero pending delivery and retains every admitted final Envelope, and both operations retain the configured audit tail before compacting their source stream. Compacted channel snapshots and audit pages disclose their first retained cursor so a consumer can resume from the retained boundary without guessing. Internal scheduler and recovery code keeps the separate full-read methods because it owns complete replay and repair.

The Hub discovers Team journals through `ctx.storageLog.list()`, validates a checkpoint watermark, and replays the suffix. Checkpointed activation fences must fall within the Team lifetime and be at or after the accepted cancellation request or completion/failure intent; a cancellation fence may precede its later terminal closure. Final-admission timestamps must fall within the Team lifetime, and workflow checkpoints validate both task-to-plan and plan-to-task references plus attached channel identities. When both grants are retained, checkpointed Participant authority must fit within its Team grant across operations, workspace modes, read/write scopes, and budgets. Checkpointed task owner proposals must name a retained Team participant; a departed participant remains a valid historical reference. Inconsistent checkpoints fall back to their journals; incomplete but consistent compiling bindings remain recoverable. Team journals use format 30 and Team checkpoints use format 31; channel WALs use format 5 and channel checkpoints use format 8; audit projections use format 1. Earlier pre-release formats are rejected. `recoveryPageSize`, `checkpointEvery`, `auditRetentionTail`, `disposalTimeoutMs`, pending-delivery and retry-key capacity, depth, lease, and budget limits are deployment configuration. Team and channel compaction preserve original cursors and reject old reads explicitly after the retained prefix is removed. A completed projected task must match its final attempt's completed result; an explicit projected failure must match its final attempt's failure, while omitted failure detail remains valid.

Terminal recovery checks attached channel ownership, terminal phase, and zero pending deliveries against the authoritative WALs before exposing a Team from a checkpoint or journal. Completed recovery additionally checks the selected final's admission, retained Envelope, fingerprint, human recipient, and receipt. Final lookups honor the retained WAL prefix. Invalid checkpoint relationships permit source-journal fallback; storage and exact-implementation loading failures propagate to the caller.

On unload, the Hub synchronously closes admission and cursor watches, waits for accepted work within `disposalTimeoutMs`, attempts final checkpoints, and closes every loaded stream before reporting aggregate cleanup failures. `usageRates` is an optional provider/model table of per-token cost units; it is copied into each Team's `rules` at creation so a rate edit cannot reinterpret historical usage.

When a typed Team, task, or authority grant sets `maxCostUnits`, a usage sample or propagated child charge without explicit cost must resolve through that frozen rate table; unknown pricing is rejected rather than counted as zero.

Workflow plans are admitted as one complete validated JSON value and remain `compiling` until every task template and its plan-owned versioned workflow channel are durably bound. Task and channel retries use the plan/template identity, and the scheduler ignores plan tasks until the plan is `ready`; restart therefore continues from journal records without rerunning model-written code.

## Model Experience

### Local durable authority

#### What the model sees

`ctx.teams` has no direct model-visible content. This provider registers no prompt section or tool; a later Team client or product Consumer owns all model-visible Team input and output.

#### Token effect

Zero direct token effect.

#### KV Cache effect

No request prefix is owned by this package.

## Known Limitations and Deferred Work

- **No participant execution or Link transport** — the Hub persists activation identity and residency but does not create or own AgentRuntime handles, remote endpoints, remote reconnect, or transport retry.
- **No scheduler, task-delivery publisher, workspace, or product entry point** — the Hub persists task attempts and validates delivery-bound starts but does not select owners, append task-assignment Envelopes, allocate workspaces, or provide API, SDK, UI, or default-bundle composition.

The [local Team Hub decision](../../../.agents/notes/implemented/architecture/2026-08-27-local-team-hub-durable-authority.md) records the durable-authority split and the [Team subsystem reference](../../../docs/subsystems/team.md) defines the public provider operations.

### Single-task cancellation

Task cancellation preserves the first authenticated intent and rejects subsequent start, heartbeat, report, or review activity. Review cleanup closes the selected attempt’s consult; scheduler reconciliation resumes interrupted cleanup. Owner acknowledgement and activation quiescence settle only after allocation release. [Cancellation ownership](../../../.agents/notes/implemented/architecture/2026-09-06-exact-single-task-cancellation.md).

`rankingLatencyBucketsMs` and `rankingCostRateBuckets` freeze `rules.taskRanking` at Team creation. `ParticipantStats.taskOutcomes` exposes per-capability integer counts and fixed-size latency histograms derived from settled attempts; task deletion and journal compaction retain their source history.
